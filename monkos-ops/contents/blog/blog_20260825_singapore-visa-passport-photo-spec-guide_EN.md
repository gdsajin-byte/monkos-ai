# Singapore Visa Photo Requirements (2026): the 60KB Rule Nobody Warns You About

If you're preparing documents for Singapore, the photo spec looks reassuringly familiar: **35 × 45mm**, white background, neutral expression. The same size used across much of Asia and Europe. So most people grab a photo they already have and upload it.

Then it gets rejected — **and almost never because of the size.**

Singapore's ICA (Immigration & Checkpoints Authority) is relaxed about the frame and strict about three things most countries don't emphasize. This guide covers exactly those three.

---

## Quick reference — Singapore photo specs (ICA)

| Item | Passport | Visa |
|------|----------|------|
| Print size | 35 × 45mm | 35 × 45mm |
| Pixels | 400 × 514px | 413 × 531px |
| Resolution | 300dpi | 300dpi |
| Background | White | White |
| Face height | 25 – 35mm | 25 – 35mm |
| Taken within | 3 months | 3 months |
| File | JPEG, **max 60KB** | **max 60KB** |
| Authority | ICA | ICA |

Only the print-size row matches what you're used to. Every other row is where rejections actually happen.

---

## Gate 1: the 60KB file cap — the #1 blocker

This is Singapore's most unusual requirement: **JPEG, 60KB or smaller.**

For scale: a typical smartphone photo is **3–5MB** — fifty to eighty times over the limit. Even the high-resolution file from a photo studio won't upload as-is.

Two failure patterns follow:

- Perfect photo, correct background, right pose → **the upload button simply refuses it**
- Panic-compress it with a random app → the file fits, but the face turns to mush → **rejected for image quality**

The second one is the real trap. **Getting under 60KB is easy; keeping the face sharp under 60KB is not.**

> 💡 **The trick is the order of operations.** Crop to the exact pixel spec first — 400 × 514px for passport — and *then* compress. A pre-cropped image reaches 60KB with far less damage than compressing a 4000px original. Do it backwards and you only lose quality.

Also note the passport and visa **pixel specs are different** (400×514 vs 413×531) despite the identical print size. Don't reuse the passport file for a visa application.

---

## Gate 2: taken within 3 months — not 6

Most countries accept photos taken within **6 months**. Singapore cuts that in half: **3 months.**

This bites people who had a batch of photos printed and have been using them across applications. A four-month-old photo that's still valid everywhere else fails the Singapore recency rule — even though nothing is wrong with the photo itself.

> ✅ **Check the date it was taken.** Past 90 days? Retake it, no matter how compliant it looks.

---

## Gate 3: face height 25–35mm — tight-framed photos exceed it

Studio conventions in much of East Asia frame the face large — commonly **32–36mm** from crown to chin. Singapore's allowed range is **25–35mm**, so a tightly framed photo can sit right at, or just past, the upper limit.

| | Common studio framing | Singapore (ICA) |
|---|---|---|
| Face height | 32 – 36mm | **25 – 35mm** |
| Headroom | tight | **generous** |

The range itself is wide — the safe move is simply to **leave a little more room above the head** than you're used to. If the framing feels slightly loose, it's probably exactly right for ICA.

---

## The 5 rejection reasons ICA actually flags

Even with every number correct, these still fail applications:

1. **Uneven background** — a white wall photographed under one-sided lighting reads as grey blotches.
2. **Tilted head** — camera angle tilts you even when you feel perfectly straight.
3. **Size mismatch** — the pixel and face-height issues above.
4. **Shadows** — standing against the wall throws a shadow behind the neck and shoulders. **Take one step forward.**
5. **Expression** — eyes straight at the camera, mouth closed, no smile. Even a slight smile fails.

Numbers 1 and 4 share one cause: lighting and wall distance. Fix those two and home-shot photos pass at a much higher rate.

## Can you take it at home?

Yes — ICA doesn't require studio photography. **A self-taken photo is valid if it meets the spec.** The hard part is meeting all of it by hand:

- a **uniformly** white background
- an **exact** crop to 400×514px (or 413×531px)
- face height inside 25–35mm
- compressed to **under 60KB** with the face still sharp

## Three-line summary

1. **The 35×45mm size is the familiar part** — don't let it lull you into skipping the rest.
2. **The real gate is 60KB.** Crop to spec first, compress second — the reverse order only costs quality.
3. **3-month recency, 35mm face-height ceiling.** Both are stricter than the habits most of us bring from other countries' specs.

Singapore isn't a "hard-spec" country — it's a country that **checks different things**. Verify these three and you'll pass on the first try.
